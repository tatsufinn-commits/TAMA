## 1. THE PHILIPPINE CLIMATE (why tropical design exists)

- **Type:** warm-humid (tropical monsoon/rainforest) — high temperature year-round, high humidity, heavy rainfall, typhoons.
- **Design imperatives:** (1) reduce heat gain; (2) promote air movement (ventilation is the main cooling strategy — humidity makes evaporative cooling less effective); (3) rain/typhoon resilience; (4) daylight without glare/heat.
- **Key data (typical):** temperature 25–32°C; relative humidity 70–90%; prevailing winds vary seasonally (Amihan NE, Habagat SW) — orientation must consider both.

## 2. PASSIVE DESIGN STRATEGIES (the exam core — grouped)

### 2.1 Passive design by Building Configuration & Orientation
- **Orientation:** elongate the building along the E–W axis; main façades face N–S (minimize low-angle east/west sun); orient openings to prevailing winds (openings at 90° to wind = max pressure/velocity).
- **Configuration:** shallow plan depth (≤ ~6–8 m typical guidance) for cross-ventilation; courtyards/light wells for air + light; raised ground floor (flood/ventilation — vernacular precedent).

### 2.2 Façade & Envelope
- **Shading devices:** roof overhangs/eaves, deep balconies/verandas, louvers (fixed/operable), brise-soleil, sun-breakers, perforated screens.
- **Window design:** large operable openings (not fixed glass); high-level openings for heat escape (stack effect); insect screening that still allows airflow.
- **Roof:** high ceiling/ventilated roof space (insulation + air gap), light-colored reflective roof, steep pitch for rain; overhangs protect walls.
- **Wall:** light-colored/low-thermal-mass where possible (night-cooling matters more than thermal lag in warm-humid), ventilated cavities.

### 2.3 Natural Ventilation Principles
- **Cross-ventilation:** air moves from positive to negative pressure zone; inlet low + outlet high creates flow; avoid partitions blocking the path.
- **Stack effect (chimney effect):** hot air rises in a vertical shaft → draws cooler air in at low level; roof monitors/ventilating ridges exploit it.
- **Wind scooping/channeling:** landscaping, wing walls, building form to capture/canalize breezes.

### 2.4 Solar Control Devices (glazing treatments)
- Shade glass before treating it: overhangs/louvers first.
- **Low-E glazing** — coatings reduce heat transfer while passing visible light.
- **Double glazing / dynamic louvres** — second layer with operable louvres (mainly for mixed-mode/acoustic contexts in PH).
- **Smart/photochromic glass** — responsive tinting (advanced/niche in PH practice — label as such).

## 3. THE VERNACULAR PRECEDENT (ties to PH Architecture file)

| Vernacular device | Passive principle |
|---|---|
| Bahay kubo: stilts, steep thatch, sawali walls | Flood/ventilation; rain runoff; permeable walls |
| Bahay na bato: stone base, timber top, capiz windows, ventanillas, volada, deep eaves | Mass where needed, permeability where it counts; stack + cross ventilation; shading |
| Torogan (Maranao) | Raised hall, deep overhangs, carved ventilation openings |
| Modern synthesis: Locsin's floating volumes, Mañosa's neo-vernacular | Shade + breeze + national identity (PH modernism) |

**Exam angle:** "Explain how the bahay na bato achieves thermal comfort passively" — the classic essay.

## 4. TROPICAL DESIGN vs MECHANICAL COOLING

- Passive first; mechanical (AC) supplements for deep-plan/special spaces (BU2/3 link — HVAC loads reduce with good envelope/orientation).
- **Green building links:** BERDE/LEED credit orientation, ventilation, daylighting; energy-efficiency compliance (PH Green Building Code referral — verify current).

## 5. COMMON MISTAKES

| Trap | Fix |
|---|---|
| Western (heating-dominated) design assumptions | PH cooling-dominated: prioritize shade + airflow, not thermal mass retention |
| Big fixed glass walls | Operable openings + external shading (not internal blinds) |
| Ignoring prevailing-wind direction (both seasons) | Orient for Amihan + Habagat; don't block cross paths with interior walls |
| East/west glass | Minimize E/W fenestration or shade heavily |
| Low ceilings with AC-only mindset | High ceilings/vented roof for stack effect (hybrid operation) |
| Treating tropical design as "just eaves" | It's a whole-system approach (orientation, form, envelope, ventilation, materials) |
