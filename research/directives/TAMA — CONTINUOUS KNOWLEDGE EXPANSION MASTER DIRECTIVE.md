# TAMA — CONTINUOUS KNOWLEDGE EXPANSION MASTER DIRECTIVE

## MISSION: BUILD THE ACADEMIC BRAIN

You are continuing development of **TAMA**, not starting a new project.

Your current objective is to **systematically expand the TAMA Knowledge Vault until every course and knowledge domain represented in the Vault has a deep, well-sourced, academically useful knowledge corpus.**

The Vault is currently incomplete.

Do **not** interpret the existence of:

- `VAULT-INDEX`
- `KNOWLEDGE-COVERAGE-MATRIX`
- acquisition logs
- research reports
- source registries
- existing curated material

as evidence that the knowledge acquisition mission is finished.

Those systems are your **memory, map, and control system**.

Your task is to use them to determine what TAMA already knows, what it partially knows, what it does not know, and what it should learn next.

---

# 1. CORE OBJECTIVE

Your objective is:

> **Populate the TAMA Vault with as much high-quality, relevant, verified, academically useful knowledge as reasonably possible for every course and subject domain represented within the Vault.**

Do not merely collect documents.

Build **usable knowledge**.

The final Vault should be capable of supporting:

- teaching
- tutoring
- explanation
- review
- examination
- active recall
- research
- architectural design reasoning
- technical problem solving
- Philippine architectural practice
- interdisciplinary learning
- future AI agents

The goal is not:

> "Have a lot of files."

The goal is:

> **"Have a comprehensive academic knowledge foundation that TAMA's agents can reliably reason from."**

# 6. THE 15-LAYER KNOWLEDGE MODEL

For every course, investigate the following layers.

## Layer 1 — CURRICULUM

Determine:

- what the course teaches
- what students are expected to learn
- prerequisites
- learning outcomes
- major subject areas
- relationship to other courses

Prefer authoritative university/curriculum sources.

---

## Layer 2 — FUNDAMENTALS

Find the foundational knowledge required to understand the course.

Include:

- definitions
- terminology
- principles
- theories
- concepts
- classifications
- basic relationships

Ask:

> "What would a beginner need to know before they could understand this subject?"

---

## Layer 3 — TEXTBOOK KNOWLEDGE

Research established academic literature.

Look for:

- textbooks
- reference books
- academic handbooks
- established authors
- university reading lists
- bibliographies
- foundational works

Do not attempt to illegally acquire copyrighted books.

Where full acquisition is not legitimate, preserve:

- bibliographic information
- official source
- relevant metadata
- recommended chapters
- legitimate summaries/references

---

## Layer 4 — UNIVERSITY MATERIAL

Search for legitimate educational resources from:

- universities
- professors
- academic institutions
- open courseware
- publicly available lectures
- lecture notes
- presentations
- modules

PowerPoints and lecture materials are particularly valuable when legitimately accessible.

---

## Layer 5 — ACADEMIC RESEARCH

Search for:

- peer-reviewed papers
- journals
- conference papers
- theses
- dissertations
- research reports
- systematic reviews
- meta-analyses

Use research to deepen the knowledge beyond introductory textbooks.

---

## Layer 6 — PROFESSIONAL PRACTICE

Determine how the knowledge is actually applied.

Look for:

- professional guides
- technical manuals
- industry publications
- professional organizations
- technical documentation
- construction practices
- architectural practice resources

The objective is to prevent TAMA from becoming purely theoretical.

---

## Layer 7 — PHILIPPINE CONTEXT

For architecture-related subjects, actively investigate Philippine relevance.

Search for:

- Philippine laws
- building codes
- regulations
- standards
- professional practice
- local case studies
- Philippine architecture
- climate
- construction practices
- local materials
- local planning
- local environmental conditions

When applicable, prioritize official Philippine sources.

---

## Layer 8 — CASE STUDIES

Find real-world examples.

For architecture and design courses, prioritize:

- buildings
- projects
- architects
- sites
- construction examples
- urban developments
- historical examples
- failures
- successful implementations

Extract:

- context
- design decisions
- constraints
- solutions
- outcomes
- lessons

---

## Layer 9 — VISUAL KNOWLEDGE

Where legitimately accessible, search for:

- diagrams
- architectural drawings
- technical drawings
- charts
- schematics
- presentations
- photographs
- illustrations
- construction details
- explanatory graphics

Visual information is especially important for architectural education.

Do not simply download images without provenance.

Record their source.

---

## Layer 10 — EXAMPLES & EXERCISES

Find:

- worked examples
- practice problems
- exercises
- design exercises
- calculations
- sample analyses
- demonstrations
- practical activities

These should eventually support TAMA's teaching and examination systems.

---

## Layer 11 — TERMINOLOGY

Build the vocabulary required for the course.

Record:

- technical terms
- abbreviations
- definitions
- synonyms
- professional terminology
- Philippine terminology where relevant

---

## Layer 12 — COMMON MISTAKES

Research what students and practitioners commonly misunderstand.

Identify:

- misconceptions
- incorrect assumptions
- calculation mistakes
- design mistakes
- terminology confusion
- common examination traps
- common practical errors

This information is particularly valuable to:

- `@mentor`
- `@examiner`
- `@drillmaster`

## Layer 14 — ADVANCED KNOWLEDGE

# 7. DEPTH MODEL

Use the following conceptual depth model:

```text
LEVEL 0 — UNKNOWN
No meaningful information.

LEVEL 1 — AWARENESS
Basic definitions and introductory material.

LEVEL 2 — FOUNDATIONAL
Core concepts and principles.

LEVEL 3 — FUNCTIONAL
Examples, applications, procedures, and exercises.

LEVEL 4 — ADVANCED
Research, professional practice, complex applications.

LEVEL 5 — COMPREHENSIVE
Multiple authoritative sources, interdisciplinary connections,
case studies, Philippine context, misconceptions,
applications, and advanced material.
```

The objective is to push important course domains toward:

**LEVEL 5 — COMPREHENSIVE**

Do not artificially force every tiny subtopic to Level 5.

Use judgment.

---

# 8. COVERAGE ≠ DEPTH

This is a critical rule.

If the Coverage Matrix says:

> Structures — 90%

that does NOT necessarily mean Structures is 90% understood.

Coverage should distinguish between:

```text
BREADTH
DEPTH
SOURCE QUALITY
SOURCE DIVERSITY
PHILIPPINE RELEVANCE
PRACTICAL APPLICATION
ACADEMIC AUTHORITY
```

A topic with one shallow webpage should not receive the same confidence as a topic supported by:

- textbooks
- university material
- academic research
- professional documentation
- Philippine regulations

# 31. DIMINISHING RETURNS

The objective is comprehensive coverage, but research has a natural stopping point.

A topic can be considered sufficiently researched when:

- foundational knowledge is covered
- important concepts are covered
- authoritative sources exist
- relevant academic research is represented
- practical applications are represented
- Philippine context is represented where relevant
- important misconceptions are understood
- cross-disciplinary relationships are known
- further searching produces mostly repetitive information

At that point:

**move to the next knowledge gap.**

Do not waste time collecting infinite duplicates.
