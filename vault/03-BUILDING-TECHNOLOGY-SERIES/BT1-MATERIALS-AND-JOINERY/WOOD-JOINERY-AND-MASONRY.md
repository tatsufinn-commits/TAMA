## 1. THEORETICAL & METHODOLOGICAL FOUNDATIONS

### 1.1 Anisotropic Physics of Timber
Wood is an organic, cellular, **anisotropic** material whose physical and mechanical properties vary significantly along three principal axes:
* **Longitudinal (Parallel to grain):** Highest tensile and compressive strength; minimal shrinkage/swelling ($0.1\%$).
* **Radial (Perpendicular to growth rings):** Intermediate strength; moderate shrinkage ($3\%–5\%$).
* **Tangential (Tangent to growth rings):** Lowest strength; highest shrinkage/swelling ($6\%–10\%$), causing warping and cupping during seasoning.

```

                ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
                │ TANGENTIAL SHRINKAGE (6-10%) >> RADIAL SHRINKAGE (3-5%)     │
                │ >> LONGITUDINAL SHRINKAGE (0.1%)                            │
                └──────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────┘
                                               │
               ┌───────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────┐
               ▼                                                               ▼
  ┌─────────────────────────────┐                               ┌─────────────────────────────┐
  │   ANNUAL GROWTH RINGS       │                               │   SEASONING WARPING MODES   │
  │ • Springwood (Rapid growth) │                               │ • Cupping (Curved cross-sec)│

```

### 1.2 Fiber Saturation Point (FSP) & Equilibrium Moisture Content (EMC)
* **Fiber Saturation Point (FSP):** Occurs at approximately **$28\% – 30\%$ moisture content (MC)** when all free water inside cell cavities has evaporated, but bound water remains in cell walls. Timber shrinkage only begins below FSP.
* **Equilibrium Moisture Content in the Philippines:** Due to high ambient relative humidity ($75\%–85\%$), the EMC of seasoned architectural timber in the Philippines stabilizes at **$12\% – 16\%$ MC**.

## 2. TECHNICAL MECHANICS, TIMBER CLASSIFICATION & JOINERY DETAILS

### 2.1 Philippine Commercial Hardwood Classification (FPRDI / NSCP 2015)
Philippine timbers are grouped into 4 strength and durability classes based on Forest Products Research and Development Institute (FPRDI) standards:

| Timber Group | Representative Philippine Species | Mechanical Properties | Primary Architectural & Structural Uses |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **High Strength & High Durability (Group 1)** | **Yakal, Molave, Guijo, Ipil, Narra, Tindalo** | Bending stress $f_b \ge 16.5\text{ MPa}$, extreme termite/decay resistance, dense grain. | Heavy timber columns, foundation piles, bridge timbers, external door thresholds, fine furniture (Narra). |
| **Medium Strength & Durability (Group 2)** | **Apitong, Kamagong, Tanguile, Red Lauan** | $f_b \approx 11.0 – 15.0\text{ MPa}$, good strength, requires preservative pressure treatment. | Roof trusses, floor joists, purlins, structural rafters, interior door frames. |
| **Moderately Light / Framing (Group 3)** | **White Lauan, Almon, Bagtikan, Mayapis** | $f_b \approx 8.0 – 10.5\text{ MPa}$, soft-to-medium hardwood ("Philippine Mahogany"). | Interior wall studs, ceiling joists, furniture carcasses, marine plywood veneer. |
| **Light & Softwood (Group 4)** | **Falcata, Benguet Pine, Gmelina** | $f_b < 7.5\text{ MPa}$, fast-growing plantation species, low decay resistance. | Formwork lumber, pallets, temporary staging, acoustic backing cores. |

### 2.2 Traditional Architectural Wood Joinery Details
* **Mortise and Tenon Joint:** One member features a projecting rectangular peg (tenon) inserted into a matching carved socket (mortise). Highly resistant to rotational torsion and shear (used in timber framing and panel door stiles/rails).
* **Dovetail Joint:** Interlocking trapezoidal pins and tails. Provides immense tensile pull-out resistance without mechanical fasteners (cabinet drawer fronts, box corners).
* **Scarf Joint:** Tapered beveled splice reinforced with fish plates and thru-bolts, used to splice and extend long-span timber beams horizontally.
* **Lap Joint (Half-Lap):** Half the thickness of each piece is removed so they intersect flush. Requires glue, nails, or wood dowels.

## 4. WORKED MATHEMATICAL DERIVATIONS & ESTIMATING CALCULATIONS

### 4.1 Timber Board Foot ($Bd.Ft.$) Sizing & Volume Calculation
**Problem**: An architectural timber roof pergola requires 12 pieces of Guijo rafters ($2" \times 6" \times 16\text{ ft}$) and 24 pieces of Yakal purlins ($2" \times 3" \times 12\text{ ft}$). Determine total Board Feet ($Bd.Ft.$) and total procurement cost at $\text{Php 95.00 per Bd.Ft.}$.

```
Step 1: Formula for Timber Board Foot (Imperial Dimension Formula)
1 Board Foot (Bd.Ft.) = [ Thickness (inches) × Width (inches) × Length (feet) ] / 12

Step 2: Calculate Guijo Rafters (12 pcs - 2" × 6" × 16')
Bd.Ft. per piece = (2 × 6 × 16) / 12 = 192 / 12 = 16.0 Bd.Ft.
Total Rafter Board Feet = 12 pcs × 16.0 Bd.Ft./pc = 192.0 Bd.Ft.

Step 3: Calculate Yakal Purlins (24 pcs - 2" × 3" × 12')
Bd.Ft. per piece = (2 × 3 × 12) / 12 = 72 / 12 = 6.0 Bd.Ft.
Total Purlin Board Feet = 24 pcs × 6.0 Bd.Ft./pc = 144.0 Bd.Ft.

Step 4: Total Lumber Requirement & Financial Cost
Total Board Footage = 192.0 + 144.0 = 336.0 Bd.Ft.
Add 10% cutting and dressing allowance = 336.0 × 1.10 = 369.6 Bd.Ft.
Total Material Cost = 369.6 Bd.Ft. × Php 95.00/Bd.Ft. = Php 35,112.00.
```

## 6. SOCRATIC EXAM SIMULATION (BOARD-STYLE SCENARIOS)

#### Question 1 (Philippine Hardwood Identification):
Which premier Philippine hardwood is characterized by its distinct yellowish-brown hue, exceptional resistance to marine borers and subterranean termites, and is legally mandated as the standard timber species for exposed structural house posts (*haligi*) and bridge timbers?
- A) Almon
- B) Yakal
- C) Falcata
- D) White Lauan
> **Correct Answer: B**  
> **Distractor Trap Analysis**:
> * Yakal (*Shorea astylosa*) is a Class 1 heavy hardwood exhibiting superior bending strength ($f_b \ge 16.5\text{ MPa}$) and natural preservative resin, making it ideal for high-stress structural posts.
> * Almon and White Lauan (Options A and D) belong to Group 3 ("Philippine Mahogany") and decay rapidly when in contact with soil/weather.
> * Falcata (Option C) is a soft plantation wood used only for paper pulp and formwork.
