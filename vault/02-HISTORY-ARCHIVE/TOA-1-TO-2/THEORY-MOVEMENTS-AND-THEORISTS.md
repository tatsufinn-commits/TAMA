## 1. WHY THEORY MATTERS (course purpose)
Theory is the "why" behind design decisions: it supplies the vocabulary, the history of ideas, and the critical frameworks that let an architect justify, critique, and innovate. TOA 1 establishes foundations (definitions, form/space, design process); TOA 2 surveys the movements and their thinkers.

## 2. THE MOVEMENT LANDSCAPE (chronological map)

### 2.1 Functionalism (19th–20th c.)
- **Core claim:** form follows function; plan/program determines form; ornament rejected.
- **Thinkers:** Louis Sullivan ("form follows function," 1896); Adolf Loos (*Ornament and Crime*, 1908); Le Corbusier (machine aesthetic).
- **Architecture:** Sullivan's Wainwright Building; Bauhaus functionalism.
- **Critique:** led to sterile "boxes"; ignores meaning, culture, symbolism (the seed of every later movement).

### 2.2 Structuralism (late 1950s–1970s)
- **Core claim:** architecture should express **invariant social/cultural structures** (patterns of human relationship, communication, collective memory) — not individual objects; the whole is a system of relationships.
- **Roots:** structural linguistics (Saussure), anthropology (Lévi-Strauss), semiotics.
- **Thinkers/architects:** Team 10 (Aldo van Eyck — the "in-between" realm, Forum 7/1959), Herman Hertzberger (Centraal Beheer — the office as a small town; user adaptation), Kenzo Tange (*Function, Structure and Symbol*, 1966), Candilis-Josic-Woods (Toulouse-le-Mirail, Berlin Free University), Aldo Rossi (meaningful permanences; the city as collective memory).
- **Key concepts:** morphology over style; hierarchy & legibility (public↔semi-public↔private); flexibility & growth ("mainframe + infill" — long-life structure, short-life infill); architecture as a rule system/grammar.
- **Distinction (exam trap):** structuralism is NOT "user-participation democracy" — it treats social patterns as invariant structures to be expressed.

### 2.3 Semiotics & Post-Structuralism (1960s–)
- **Semiotics (signs in architecture):** architecture as a language of signs — signifier/signified; icon, index, symbol (Peirce); denotation vs connotation (Barthes); Umberto Eco's "function and sign"; Winland Klassen's semiological approach (observed in TAMA TOA materials — verified).
- **Post-structuralism:** meaning is unstable, deferred (Derrida — deconstruction); no fixed signified; architecture as text.
- **Architecture:** Eisenman's houses (deconstructivist explorations); Tschumi's Parc de la Villette (disjunction, event).

### 2.4 Phenomenology (1970s–)
- **Core claim:** architecture is about **lived experience** — place, atmosphere, sensory presence; "being-in-the-world" (Heidegger).
- **Thinkers:** Christian Norberg-Schulz (*Genius Loci: Towards a Phenomenology of Place*, 1979 — the "spirit of place"); Juhani Pallasmaa (*The Eyes of the Skin* — the primacy of touch/multi-sensory experience); Gaston Bachelard (*The Poetics of Space* — the house as intimate universe).
- **Key concepts:** genius loci (spirit of place); place vs space; atmosphere; materiality/tectonics as experience.
- **Architecture:** Peter Zumthor (thermal baths at Vals — material/atmospheric architecture); Steven Holl (anchoring, parallax).

### 2.5 Critical Regionalism (1980s–)
- **Core claim:** resist placelessness and universal clichés; embrace **topography, climate, light, craft and tectonics** of the region — but critically (not nostalgic vernacular copying).
- **Thinkers:** Alexander Tzonis & Liane Lefaivre (coined the term); **Kenneth Frampton** (*Towards a Critical Regionalism: Six Points for an Architecture of Resistance*, 1983 — in *The Anti-Aesthetic*): topography over tabula rasa; tectonic over scenographic; local light/climate as expressive material.
- **Key concepts:** "building the site" (Mario Botta's phrase); place-conscious architecture; critical vs sentimental regionalism.
- **PH relevance:** Locsin's floating volume, Mañosa's neo-vernacular, Antonio's tropical modernism are PH instances of regional modernism (cross-link PHILIPPINE-ARCHITECTURE-HISTORY).

### 2.6 Postmodernism (1960s–1980s)
- **Core claim:** reaction against modernism's austerity; return of **meaning, history, ornament, symbolism, complexity**.
- **Thinkers:** Robert Venturi (*Complexity and Contradiction in Architecture*, 1966 — "Less is a bore"; *Learning from Las Vegas*, 1972 — the decorated shed vs the duck); Charles Jencks (postmodernism as "double-coding"); Aldo Rossi (typology); Michael Graves (poetics of pastiche).
- **Architecture:** Graves' Portland Building; Venturi's Vanna Venturi House (observed in TAMA TOA2 materials — verified).
- **PH note:** Postmodernism influenced PH commercial architecture of the 1980s–90s (flag as contextual, verify per building).

### 2.7 (Advanced/further) Deconstructivism, Parametricism, Critical Practice
- **Deconstructivism** (1988 MoMA show): fragmentation, non-linear geometry (Gehry, Hadid, Libeskind, Eisenman, Tschumi).
- **Parametricism** (Schumacher, 2008): algorithmic/parametric design as the contemporary style; ties to AR113S digital-architecture content (verified in courses/).
- **Critical practice / agency:** architecture as political/cultural intervention (Koolhaas, the "post-critical" debate) — label as contemporary discourse.

## 4. KEY TERMS (glossary)

- **Parti** — the central organizing idea of a design.
- **Genius loci** — spirit of place (Norberg-Schulz).
- **Tectonics** — the poetic expression of construction/structure (Frampton).
- **Typology** — the study/use of building types as carriers of meaning (Rossi).
- **Semiotics** — the study of signs and their meaning.
- **Signifier/signified** — the sign's form vs its concept (Saussure).
- **Deconstruction** — exposing unstable meanings (Derrida); ≠ "demolition."
- **Double-coding** — speaking to both elite and popular audiences (Jencks).
- **Mainframe + infill** — long-life structure + adaptable infill (Hertzberger).
- **In-between realm** — the intermediate space mediating inside/outside, public/private (van Eyck).

## 5. COMMON MISTAKES

| Trap | Fix |
|---|---|
| Structuralism = user democracy | It expresses invariant social structures (Rossi/Hertzberger debate noted) |
| Postmodernism = "more modern" | It's a reaction: history, ornament, complexity return |
| Phenomenology = "feelings only" | It's a rigorous philosophical position on lived experience (Heidegger-derived) |
| Critical regionalism = vernacular copying | It's *critical*: universal modernity inflected by region (Frampton) |
| "Less is a bore" = Mies | It's Venturi (answering Mies' "less is more") |
| Deconstruction = destruction | Deconstruction is an analytical method, not demolition |
| Genius loci = "vibe" | It's a structured concept of place-identity (Norberg-Schulz) |
