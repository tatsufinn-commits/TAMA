## 1. THEORETICAL & HISTORIOGRAPHICAL FOUNDATIONS

### 1.1 The Tripartite Historiography of Philippine Architecture
Philippine architectural identity is a continuous dialectic between indigenous vernacular austronesian traditions, colonial hybridization (*Arquitectura Mestiza*), and post-war critical regionalism:

```
                         PHILIPPINE ARCHITECTURAL HISTORIOGRAPHY
  ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │ 1. PRE-COLONIAL AUSTRONESIAN VERNACULAR: Pile-dwelling, steep thatch, open joints │
  ├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
  │ 2. SPANISH COLONIAL & ARQUITECTURA MESTIZA: Timber upper frame on stone base     │
  │    • Earthquake Baroque: Massive low buttresses, thick coral/adobe walls         │
  ├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
  │ 3. AMERICAN COLONIAL & COMMONWEALTH: Beaux-Arts classicism & City Beautiful      │
  ├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
  │ 4. POST-WAR NATION BUILDING & MODERNISM: Floating masses, folded plates, brise   │
  │    soleil, tropical regionalism (Leandro Locsin, Francisco Mañosa)               │
  └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## 2. INDIGENOUS VERNACULAR & SPANISH COLONIAL TYPOLOGIES

### 2.1 Pre-Colonial Indigenous Dwellings Matrix
| Indigenous Typology | Region & Ethnic Group | Architectural Anatomy & Environmental Adaptation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bahay Kubo** | Lowland Luzon / Visayas | Elevated on wooden posts; bamboo slat flooring (*lantay*) for passive floor ventilation; steep nipa palm roof (*bubong*); operable wall panels (*dingding*). |
| **Ifugao Fale** | Cordillera Mountains | Mortise-and-tenon interlocking timber construction erected **without a single metal nail**; pyramid cogon thatch roof; wooden cylindrical rat-guards (*halipan*) on posts. |
| **Maranao Torogan** | Lanao Lake, Mindanao | Royal aristocratic house elevated on massive round tree trunks; characterized by wing-like protruding wooden carved beams (*panolong*) with *okir* floral and naga serpent motifs. |
| **Ivatan Sinadumparan** | Batanes Islands | Thick lime-and-stone masonry walls ($0.80–1.00\text{m}$ thick) and heavy multi-layered thatched cogon roof tied down with rattan nets to withstand Category 5 super typhoons. |
| **Badjao Lepa** | Sulu Archipelago | Floating house-boats and stilt dwellings elevated directly over tidal coral reef waters. |

### 2.2 The Anatomy of the *Bahay na Bato* (Arquitectura Mestiza)
A hybrid two-storey residential typology designed to survive both tropical typhoons and severe earthquakes:
* **Ground Floor (*Zaguan*):** Heavy volcanic tuff (adobe) or coral stone masonry; used for carriages, storage, and horse stables.
* **Upper Floor (*Cuarto Principal*):** Flexible timber framing joined by mortise-and-tenon pegged joints that sway elastically during earthquakes without cracking.
* **Key Architectural Components:**
  * **Volada:** Cantilevered wooden gallery projecting over the lower stone wall, extending floor area.
  * **Ventanas & Ventanillas:** Large sliding wooden jalousie windows paired with sliding **Capiz shell (*Placuna placenta*)** panels for soft daylighting, with lower grilled louvers (*ventanillas*) at floor level for cross-ventilation.
  * **Calado:** Decorative pierced wooden fretwork panels above interior partition walls facilitating air circulation across all rooms.
  * **Caida & Sala:** Formal anteroom (*caida*) leading to the grand living hall (*sala*).
  * **Azotea:** Uncovered elevated stone terrace at the rear used for outdoor domestic activities and water gathering.

## 3. PHILIPPINE STATUTORY & HERITAGE CONSERVATION MATRIX (RA 10066)

| Heritage Designation | Statutory Classification (RA 10066) | Legal Protection & Government Oversight |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **National Cultural Treasure (NCT)** | Highest statutory heritage category (e.g. San Agustin Church, Paoay Church). | Absolute state protection against demolition, alteration, or export; mandatory conservation subsidies from NCCA/National Museum. |
| **Important Cultural Property (ICP)** | Works of National Artists for Architecture (Locsin, Mañosa, Nakpil, Santos, Zaragoza) or structures $\ge \mathbf{50\text{ Years Old}}$. | Presumed Important Cultural Property; protected by **National Registry of Cultural Property (PRECUP)** against unauthorized alteration. |

## 4. NATIONAL ARTISTS FOR ARCHITECTURE & LANDMARK WORKS

| National Artist for Architecture | Year Conferred | Architectural Philosophy & Landmark Masterpieces |
| :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **Juan F. Nakpil** | 1973 (First National Artist) | Pioneer of Philippine modernist integration; designed **UP Diliman Quezon Hall**, Quiapo Church Reconstruction, Manila Jockey Club. |
| **Pablo S. Antonio** | 1976 | Master of Philippine Art Deco and tropical climate adaptation; designed **Far Eastern University (FEU) Campus**, Ideal Theater, Galaxy Theater. |
| **Leandro V. Locsin** | 1990 | Master of the "Floating Mass" and heavy sculptural concrete; designed the **Cultural Center of the Philippines (CCP) Complex**, PICC, Church of the Holy Sacrifice (UP Diliman dome), Istana Nurul Iman. |
| **Ildefonso P. Santos Jr.** | 2006 | Father of Philippine Landscape Architecture; designed **Paco Park**, Tagaytay Highlands, CCP Open Grounds, Nayong Pilipino. |
| **José María Zaragoza** | 2014 | Master of ecclesiastical modernism combining Liturgical Arts with Spanish-Filipino heritage; designed **Santo Domingo Church (QC)**, Quiapo Church expansion. |
| **Francisco "Bobby" Mañosa** | 2018 | Champion of Philippine Neo-Vernacular Architecture and indigenous materiality (bamboo, coconut wood, thatch); designed **The Coconut Palace (Tahanang Pilipino)**, San Miguel Corporation Head Office (Banaue Rice Terraces motif), Amanpulo Resort. |

## 5. SOCRATIC EXAM SIMULATION (BOARD-STYLE SCENARIOS)

#### Question 1 (Philippine Vernacular Structural Components):
In the royal ancestral dwelling of the Maranao people (*Torogan*), what is the specific indigenous architectural term for the elaborately carved, wing-like horizontal beam ends extending outward from the facade adorned with *okir* serpent and floral motifs?
- A) Calado
- B) Panolong
- C) Halipan
- D) Volada
> **Correct Answer: B**  
> **Distractor Trap Analysis**:
> * The **Panolong** is the richly carved cantilevered end of the floor beam (*tinai a walai*) in a Maranao *Torogan*, symbolizing prestige and royal status.
> * **Halipan** (Option C) is the cylindrical wooden rat-guard on the four posts of an Ifugao *Fale*.
> * **Volada** (Option D) is the cantilevered balcony gallery in a Spanish colonial *Bahay na Bato*.
> * **Calado** (Option A) is the pierced fretwork screen above interior doors in colonial homes.
