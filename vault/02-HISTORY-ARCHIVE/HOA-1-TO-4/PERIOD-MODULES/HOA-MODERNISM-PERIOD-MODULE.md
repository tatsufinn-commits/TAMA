## 1. PERIOD → CONTEXT

- **Time:** late 19th-century roots → 1910s–1960s (the "Modern Movement"); international by mid-century.
- **Context:** industrialization; new materials (steel, reinforced concrete, plate glass); urbanization; rejection of historical revivalism; the machine age; postwar reconstruction; Bauhaus and CIAM (Congrès Internationaux d'Architecture Moderne).
- **Philosophy:** "form follows function" (Louis Sullivan); honesty of materials/structure; architecture as social reform; the house as "a machine for living in" (Le Corbusier).

## 2. CHARACTERISTICS (the modern language)

1. **Functionalism** — plan/program drives form; ornament rejected ("ornament is crime" — Adolf Loos).
2. **Honest expression of structure & materials** — exposed concrete, steel, glass; no sham facades (Ruskin's Truth, industrialized).
3. **Free plan / free façade** — frame structure liberates walls from load-bearing (Le Corbusier's 5 points).
4. **Industrial materials** — reinforced concrete, steel frames, curtain walls, large glazing.
5. **Flat roofs, horizontal lines, cubic masses** — asymmetry, volume over mass (Giedion), the "international style" (Johnson-Hitchcock).
6. **New building types** — skyscraper, factory, social housing (Existenzminimum), the modern museum.

## 3. PIONEERS & MASTERS → WORKS (the exam core)

| Master | Dates | Key works | Signature idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Louis Sullivan** | 1856–1924 | Wainwright Building; Guaranty Building | "Form follows function"; the skyscraper's vertical expression |
| **Frank Lloyd Wright** | 1867–1959 | Robie House; Fallingwater; Guggenheim NY; Taliesin | Organic architecture; prairie house; integration with site |
| **Adolf Loos** | 1870–1933 | Looshaus Vienna; Villa Müller | "Ornament is crime"; Raumplan |
| **Walter Gropius** | 1883–1969 | Bauhaus Dessau; Fagus Factory | Bauhaus school; design education reform; glass curtain wall pioneer |
| **Ludwig Mies van der Rohe** | 1886–1969 | Barcelona Pavilion; Seagram Building; Farnsworth House | "Less is more"; universal space; steel+glass precision |
| **Le Corbusier** | 1887–1965 | Villa Savoye; Unité d'Habitation; Chandigarh; Ronchamp | 5 points of architecture; pilotis; machine aesthetic; modular |
| **Alvar Aalto** | 1898–1976 | Paimio Sanatorium; Finlandia Hall | Humanized modernism; organic form; timber |
| **Oscar Niemeyer** | 1907–2012 | Brasília (with Lúcio Costa); Niterói Museum | Sculptural concrete modernism |

**5 Points of Architecture (Le Corbusier — memorized classic):**
1. Pilotis (free-standing columns raising the volume)
2. Roof garden
3. Free plan (non-load-bearing interior partitions)
4. Ribbon (horizontal) window
5. Free façade (non-structural wall)

## 4. MOVEMENTS WITHIN MODERNISM (chronology trap)

- **Arts & Crafts / proto-modern (19th c.):** Morris, Ruskin — reaction to industrialization (theory link).
- **Art Nouveau (1890s–1900s):** curvilinear; Gaudi, Horta — *not* yet "modern" (decorative).
- **Expressionism:** Mendelsohn (Einstein Tower) — dynamic form.
- **De Stijl (1917–):** Mondrian, Rietveld (Schröder House) — abstract geometry.
- **Bauhaus (1919–1933):** Gropius, Mies, Klee/Kandinsky — art+technology synthesis.
- **International Style (1930s–):** Mies, Gropius, Corb's white villas — the "sterilized" modern label.
- **Organic/regional modernism:** Wright, Aalto, later Utzon — humanized counterpoint.
- **Postmodernism follows (1960s–):** Venturi ("Less is a bore"), Jencks — reaction (ties to AR132-1 TOA2 postmodern content — verified in courses/).

## 5. EXAM TRAPS

| Trap | Fix |
|---|---|
| "Modernism = Art Nouveau" | Art Nouveau is proto-modern/decorative; modernism rejects ornament |
| Confusing Wright (organic) with Mies (universal space) | Match philosophy → architect |
| "Form follows function" → Corb | It's Sullivan's phrase |
| 5 points misordered | pilotis/roof garden/free plan/ribbon window/free façade |
| Bauhaus = a style only | It's a school/movement: art+technology; Dessau building = Gropius |
| Postmodernism as modernism | Chronology: modern → postmodern reaction (Venturi, Graves — TOA2 verified) |
| PH: Locsin/Mañosa as "pure international style" | They adapt modernism to tropical/vernacular (regional modernism) |

## 6. INFLUENCE
