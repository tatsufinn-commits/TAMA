## 1. PERIOD → CONTEXT

- **Time:** mid-12th to 15th century (France: c. 1140 Saint-Denis choir onward; Europe-wide through Late Gothic).
- **Context:** High Middle Ages; cathedral cities; scholastic theology; pilgrimage; guilds; rising wealth; the "age of faith."
- **Origin:** Abbey church of **Saint-Denis** (Abbot Suger, c. 1140) — light as divine; first fully Gothic structure.

## 2. CHARACTERISTICS (the 5 structural innovations — the core exam content)

1. **Pointed (ogival) arch** — produces **less lateral thrust** than the semicircular arch; permits **variable spans** (vaulting over non-square bays); strong vertical visual line. *Not a Gothic invention* (Islamic precedent) — Gothic = combining elements into a new system.
2. **Ribbed vault** — diagonal ribs carry the vault's weight along defined lines; centering needed only for ribs, not webs → **faster, cheaper construction**; replaced Romanesque barrel/groin vaults.
3. **Flying buttress** — transfers roof/vault **lateral thrust outward** to detached piers → walls can open up; enables tall, thin walls with huge windows.
4. **Large stained-glass windows / rose windows** — walls as "glass screens"; colored light = theological symbolism (heavenly Jerusalem, Revelation imagery).
5. **Vertical emphasis** — clustered columns, pinnacles, spires, tracery; the whole composition soars.

**Structural logic (memorize this chain):** pointed arch + rib vault + flying buttress → skeleton-stone construction → less wall → more glass → taller + lighter interior.

## 3. KEY WORKS (architect → building → feature)

| Building | Location | Key feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Saint-Denis** | Paris | First Gothic choir (Suger); pointed vaults + stained glass origin |
| **Chartres Cathedral** | Chartres | High Gothic; famous stained glass; differing tower spires; flying buttresses |
| **Notre-Dame de Paris** | Paris | Early Gothic; rose windows; flying buttresses; gallery of kings |

**PH connection:** Gothic Revival appears in Philippine churches of the American era (e.g., Manila Cathedral reconstructions — Neo-Romanesque/eclectic mix; flag as context, verify per building).

## 4. TYPES / SUBDIVISIONS (England/France)

- **Early Gothic** (12th c.): Saint-Denis, Notre-Dame.
- **High Gothic** (13th c.): Chartres, Amiens, Reims.
- **Late Gothic / Flamboyant & Perpendicular:** intricate tracery (Flamboyant, France), Perpendicular vertical lines (England).
- English period names differ: Early English → Decorated → Perpendicular (trap: don't use French sequence for English buildings).

## 5. EXAM TRAPS (tied to vault trap file)

- **Trap:** "Gothic invented the pointed arch" — WRONG: Islamic/earlier precedents; Gothic = the *combination*.
- **Trap:** confusing **rib vault** (Gothic) with **groin vault** (Romanesque).
- **Trap:** attributing flying buttresses as decorative — they are **structural** (thrust transfer).
- **Trap:** Saint-Denis vs Notre-Dame (first-Gothic vs early-Gothic Parisian cathedral).
- **Trap:** English period sequence (Early English/Decorated/Perpendicular) vs French (Early/High/Late).

## 6. INFLUENCE (the "why it matters")

- Revived in the **Gothic Revival** (19th c.; Ruskin's *Seven Lamps* — see PUBLIC-DOMAIN-THEORY-NOTES; Pugin; Viollet-le-Duc restoration theory).
- Structural rationalism lineage: Gothic skeleton → modern framed structures conceptually (Giedion's narrative).
- Ruskin: Gothic = the "Truthful" style (honest structure/materials) — theory link.
