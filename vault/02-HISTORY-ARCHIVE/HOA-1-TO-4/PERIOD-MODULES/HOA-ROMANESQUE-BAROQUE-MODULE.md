# PART A — ROMANESQUE (c. 1000–1150)

## A1. PERIOD → CONTEXT
- Post-Roman Europe; monasticism (Cluny); pilgrimage routes; feudal stability; the first pan-European style since Rome.
- "Romanesque" = "in the manner of the Romans" — round arches, masonry, basilican planning.

## A2. CHARACTERISTICS (memorize the 5)
1. **Semicircular (round) arches** — derived from Roman engineering.
2. **Massive, thick load-bearing stone walls** — fortress-like; small windows → dim interiors.
3. **Barrel vaults & groin vaults** — stone vaulting over naves; groin vault = intersection of two barrel vaults.
4. **Sturdy piers/columns; arcaded interiors** — rhythmic nave arcades; sculpted capitals.
5. **West towers & cruciform plan** — prominent west-end towers; cross-shaped plans; carved portals (tympanum sculpture).

## A3. KEY WORKS
| Building | Location | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Durham Cathedral** | England | Ribbed vaulting precursor; massive piers; begins transition to Gothic |
| **Pisa Cathedral + Leaning Tower** | Italy | Romanesque + arcaded galleries; campanile |
| **Speyer Cathedral** | Germany | Largest Romanesque; groin-vaulted nave |
| **Santiago de Compostela** | Spain | Pilgrimage church; ambulatory + radiating chapels |

## A4. EXAM TRAPS
- **Trap:** "Romanesque invented rib vaults" — Durham shows *early* ribs, but the full ribbed system is Gothic.
- **Trap:** confusing barrel (single, tunnel-like) with groin (two intersecting) vaults.
- **Trap:** Romanesque = "dark & heavy" vs Gothic = "light & tall" — know WHY (vaulting + buttressing evolution).

# PART B — BAROQUE (c. 1580–1750)

## B1. PERIOD → CONTEXT
- Counter-Reformation; Catholic church patronage; absolute monarchies; a response to Renaissance restraint — **emotion, drama, persuasion**.
- Baroque = theatrical architecture: grandeur, movement, contrast.

## B2. CHARACTERISTICS (the exam list)
1. **Drama & movement** — curved walls, oval plans, dynamic facades (concave/convex).
2. **Theatrical light** — dramatic chiaroscuro; illusionistic painted ceilings (quadratura).
3. **Rich surface treatment** — gilded statuary, twisted columns (Solomonic), stucco, polychrome marble.
4. **Grandeur & scale** — monumental axes, colonnades (Bernini's St. Peter's colonnade "embracing" the faithful).
5. **Urban drama** — piazzas, fountains, processional spaces (Piazza Navona, Trevi).
6. **French Baroque = classical restraint** — Versailles: order + grandeur (French classicism tames the Italian drama).

## B3. MASTERS → WORKS
| Master | Dates | Key works | Signature |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gian Lorenzo Bernini** | 1598–1680 | St. Peter's colonnade & baldachin; Sant'Andrea al Quirinale; Piazza Navona (Fountain of Four Rivers) | Sculptural, theatrical, papal Rome |
| **Francesco Borromini** | 1599–1667 | San Carlo alle Quattro Fontane; Sant'Ivo alla Sapienza | Geometrically daring curves; the "rebel" of Baroque |
| **Pietro da Cortona** | 1596–1669 | Santa Maria della Pace façade; Barberini ceiling | Painted illusionism + façade drama |
| **Guarino Guarini** | 1624–1683 | Chapel of the Holy Shroud, Turin | Complex geometry/dome interlacing |
| **Jules Hardouin-Mansart** (France) | 1646–1708 | Versailles (Hall of Mirrors, palace) | French classical Baroque |

## B4. EXAM TRAPS
- **Trap:** Baroque = "more Renaissance" — it *reacts against* Renaissance balance (movement vs stasis).
- **Trap:** Bernini vs Borromini — Bernini = sculpture/colonnades/theatrical; Borromini = architectural geometry/curves.
- **Trap:** French Baroque = Italian Baroque — France is classically restrained (Versailles order vs Roman drama).
- **Trap:** Baroque vs Rococo — Rococo (18th c.) = intimate, decorative, pastel interior style; Baroque = grand, structural drama.
- **Trap:** timeline — Baroque comes AFTER Mannerism (Michelangelo bridges) and BEFORE Rococo/Neoclassicism.

# PART C — THE COMPARISON TABLE (Romanesque vs Gothic vs Baroque — the classic MCQ)

| Feature | Romanesque (1000–1150) | Gothic (1150–1500) | Baroque (1580–1750) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arches | Round/semicircular | Pointed | Round & oval, heavily framed |
| Walls/windows | Thick walls, small windows | Thin walls, large stained glass | Curved walls, dramatic openings |
| Vaulting | Barrel & groin | Ribbed + flying buttresses | Domes, painted illusionistic vaults |
| Light | Dim, shadowed | Bright colored daylight | Theatrical contrast |
| Feeling | Solid, fortress-like calm | Soaring, spiritual | Emotional, grand, overwhelming |
| Example | Durham Cathedral | Notre-Dame de Paris | St. Peter's (Baroque additions), San Carlo |
