## 1. PERIOD → CONTEXT

- **Time:** early 15th to early 17th century (Italy c. 1420 → Europe-wide by 1600s).
- **Context:** the Renaissance ("rebirth") — humanism; rediscovery of classical antiquity (Vitruvius rediscovered/printed 1486; Roman ruins studied); wealthy merchant patrons (Medici); the architect as intellectual (not craftsman).
- **Origin:** **Florence** — Brunelleschi's dome of Florence Cathedral (1420–1436) as the opening act.

## 2. CHARACTERISTICS (the classical language re-established)

1. **Classical orders revived** — correct proportional use of Doric/Ionic/Corinthian (and Tuscan/Composite); columns, entablatures, pediments as a coherent grammar.
2. **Rational geometry & symmetry** — plans/façades organized on mathematical proportion; central planning (circle/square); the module as design unit.
3. **Humanist proportion** — architecture scaled to human measure (Alberti: beauty = harmony of parts = *concinnitas*); Vitruvian man ideal.
4. **Perspective & linearity** — painted/sculpted perspective; shallow layered façades (pilasters rather than engaged columns); *chiaroscuro* effects.
5. **Domes & central plans** — revived dome construction; centralized church plans (circle/square inscribed).
6. **Secular building types** — palazzo (urban palace), villa (country house), piazza design; public architecture revived.

## 3. MASTERS → WORKS (the exam core)

| Master | Dates | Key works | Signature idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Filippo Brunelleschi** | 1377–1446 | Florence Cathedral dome; Ospedale degli Innocenti; San Lorenzo; Pazzi Chapel | First Renaissance architect; linear perspective; dome without centering (herringbone brick, double shell) |
| **Leon Battista Alberti** | 1404–1472 | Santa Maria Novella façade; Palazzo Rucellai; Sant'Andrea Mantua; *De re aedificatoria* (1452) | Architect as theorist; concinnitas; written treatise |
| **Donato Bramante** | 1444–1514 | Tempietto (San Pietro in Montorio); Belvedere courtyard; St. Peter's initial plan | High Renaissance; central plan; antique grandeur |
| **Michelangelo** | 1475–1564 | St. Peter's dome (completed after his death); Laurentian Library; Campidoglio | Mannerist expressiveness; sculptural architecture |
| **Andrea Palladio** | 1508–1580 | Villa Rotonda; Villa Emo; Basilica (Palazzo della Ragione) Vicenza; San Giorgio Maggiore; *I Quattro Libri* (1570) | Venetian villas; temple-front façades; treatise → global influence (Palladianism) |

**Note (ties to vault):** Palladio's *Four Books* and Alberti's *De re aedificatoria* are linked in `PUBLIC-DOMAIN-THEORY-NOTES.md` — free primary sources.

## 4. SUB-PERIODS (chronology trap)

- **Early Renaissance (Quattrocento, 1420–1500):** Florence; Brunelleschi, Alberti; flat, linear façades.
- **High Renaissance (1500–1520s):** Rome; Bramante, Raphael; monumentality, central plans.
- **Mannerism (1520–1600):** Michelangelo; tension, ambiguity, breaking classical rules deliberately.
- **Baroque follows (17th c.)** — drama, curves, movement (Bernini, Borromini) — separate module (next).

**Trap:** calling Michelangelo "High Renaissance only" — he bridges into Mannerism; St. Peter's dome is his late work.

## 5. KEY BUILDING TYPES

- **Church:** Latin-cross or central plan; dome over crossing; pedimented façades with superimposed orders.
- **Palazzo:** rusticated ground floor, piano nobile (main floor), cornice crowning; courtyard (cortile) with arcades.
- **Villa:** symmetrical, axial, temple-front porch (Palladio); agricultural + leisure functions.
- **Piazza:** designed public space (Campidoglio, Michelangelo) — urban design revival.

## 6. EXAM TRAPS

| Trap | Fix |
|---|---|
| "Renaissance invented the dome" | Domes existed (Pantheon, Hagia Sophia) — Renaissance *re-perfected* construction (Brunelleschi) |
| Confusing Brunelleschi (dome, Florence) with Bramante (Tempietto, Rome) | Match master→city→work |
| "Renaissance = Baroque" | Renaissance: order/balance; Baroque: drama/movement — chronological + stylistic distinction |
| Forgetting the treatise tradition | Alberti 1452 & Palladio 1570 — theory link (TOA) |
| Attributing San Lorenzo to Palladio | San Lorenzo = Brunelleschi (Florence); Villa Rotonda = Palladio (Vicenza) |

## 7. INFLUENCE

- **Palladianism** → Inigo Jones (England), Thomas Jefferson (USA) — centuries of global influence.
- The treatise tradition (Alberti/Palladio) → professional architecture literature → modern theory (TOA link).
- Classical language persists into Beaux-Arts and Neoclassicism (18th–19th c. revivals — already in AR123-era content).
