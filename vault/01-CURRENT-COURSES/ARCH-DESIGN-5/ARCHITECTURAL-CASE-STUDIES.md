## 2. CASE 1 — FALLINGWATER (Frank Lloyd Wright, 1935–39, Bear Run, Pennsylvania)

- **Program:** weekend house over a waterfall for the Kaufmann family.
- **Site/context:** rocky stream, waterfall, forest — the site IS the concept.
- **Concept/parti:** "organic architecture" — the house as an extension of the rock ledge; cantilevered terraces over the waterfall; horizontality echoing the rock strata.
- **Materials/structure:** reinforced concrete cantilevers (the famous terraces — engineering controversy: deflection issues required post-construction strengthening), native stone masonry, steel/glass.
- **Environmental strategy:** orientation to views & stream sound; overhangs shade; natural materials blend.
- **Circulation/spatial:** stacked/rotated levels around the central hearth; compression → release sequences.
- **Significance/lessons:** masterwork of organic modernism; the tension between architectural vision and structural reality (cantilever deflection = cautionary tale); integration of building + nature.

## 3. CASE 2 — VILLA SAVOYE (Le Corbusier, 1928–31, Poissy, France)

- **Program:** weekend villa for the Savoye family.
- **Site:** flat rural meadow (a "field of the house") — the box floats above.
- **Concept:** the **5 points of architecture** demonstrated in one building: pilotis, roof garden, free plan, ribbon window, free façade.
- **Structure:** reinforced concrete frame → walls freed from load-bearing (the free plan/facade).
- **Circulation:** the **architectural promenade** — ramp + spiral stair moving through the volume; views composed en route.
- **Environmental:** roof garden as replaced ground; ribbon windows for light; orientation.
- **Significance/lessons:** the canonical manifesto house of the Modern Movement; car-turn radius built into the plan (machine age); later leakage problems = critique of early modern detailing (lesson: modernism's blind spots).

## 4. CASE 3 — CULTURAL CENTER OF THE PHILIPPINES (Leandro Locsin, 1969, Manila)

- **Program:** national performing-arts center (theater, galleries, offices).
- **Site:** reclaimed land along Manila Bay (part of the CCP complex).
- **Concept:** the **"floating volume"** — a massive travertine-clad block hovering over an open, reflecting ground plane (pilotis + reflecting pool); the building "floats" like a native banka (boat) or a cloud.
- **Structure:** reinforced concrete with deep trusses transferring the massive volume to few supports.
- **Environmental:** reflecting pool cools the microclimate; deep overhangs shade; the open undercroft allows sea breezes (tropical modernism — ties to TROPICAL-DESIGN file).
- **Significance/lessons:** Locsin's masterpiece; PH "tropical modernism" — international modernism adapted to climate + national identity; National Artist (1990) — ties to PHILIPPINE-ARCHITECTURE-HISTORY.

## 5. CASE 4 — COCONUT PALACE (Francisco "Bobby" Mañosa, 1978–81, Manila)

- **Program:** guest residence/state function hall (built for the 1978 IMF-World Bank meetings; later used for state functions).
- **Site:** CCP Complex, Manila Bay.
- **Concept:** **neo-vernacular / national identity** — the bahay kubo re-interpreted in modern materials; the coconut as symbol.
- **Materials:** coconut shell, coconut lumber, rattan, capiz, indigenous hardwoods — an "architecture of the Philippines."
- **Structure/form:** hexagonal coconut-shell roof (the iconic "coconut" dome), interlocking shell modules, native craft techniques.
- **Environmental:** raised, open, ventilated; indigenous materials; tropical response.
- **Significance/lessons:** Mañosa's argument for a Filipino architectural language (vs imported styles); National Artist 2018; criticized by some as symbolic/patronage architecture — a good debate case (lessons: identity vs function critique).

## 6. CASE 5 — SAN CARLO ALLE QUATTRO FONTANE (Francesco Borromini, 1638–67, Rome)

- **Program:** small church + cloister (Counter-Reformation Rome).
- **Site:** tight corner site — Borromini's geometry masters a constrained lot.
- **Concept:** **undulating oval plan** — concave/convex walls creating movement; the "serpentine" facade.
- **Structure/material:** masonry with ingenious geometric vaulting (the oval dome with interlaced coffers).
- **Significance/lessons:** the Baroque's geometric daring; small site, monumental impact; Borromini as the "rebel" architect (ties to HOA-ROMANESQUE-BAROQUE module).
