## CASE 6 — SANTO DOMINGO CHURCH (Jose Maria Zaragoza, 1954, Quezon City)

1. **Architect:** Jose Maria V. Zaragoza (National Artist 2014)
2. **Date:** 1954 (post-WWII reconstruction of the Dominican church; original in Intramuros destroyed 1941/45)
3. **Location:** Quezon City (new site after Intramuros destruction)
4. **Client:** Dominican Order
5. **Program:** parish church (large sanctuary, side chapels, choir, convent adjacency)
6. **Site:** urban lot, QC; oriented for processional entry
7. **Context:** postwar rebuilding; modernist vocabulary arriving in the PH; the Dominicans' historic Intramuros church (neoclassical) as memory
8. **Design concept (parti):** **"Old World Spanish colonial + modern international"** (per the National Artist profile) — the modern shell carries colonial-liturgical continuity: a broad sanctuary with clear sightlines, expressed structure, and a prominent campanile/belfry recalling the Spanish tradition.
9. **Materials:** reinforced concrete (modern), clean planar surfaces; integrated liturgical fittings (reredos/retablo tradition adapted)
10. **Structure:** RC frame with long-span roof over the nave — the modern structural system replaces masonry vaulting while preserving the basilican sense
11. **Environmental strategy:** deep roof overhangs/eaves for shade; high-level ventilation; tropical-adapted modernism
12. **Circulation/spatial:** axial nave → sanctuary focus; side chapels; generous narthex; processional hierarchy preserved
13. **Construction:** modern methods; postwar materials economy
14. **Significance/lessons:** Zaragoza's fusion thesis — modern construction, colonial-liturgical continuity; a model for "modern church" design in the PH; National Artist recognition (2014). Lesson: modernity ≠ loss of tradition; structure + liturgy can co-evolve.

## CASE 7 — FAR EASTERN UNIVERSITY CAMPUS (Pablo Antonio, 1930s–1950s, Manila)

1. **Architect:** Pablo S. Antonio (National Artist 1976 — "Foremost Filipino Modernist of his time")
2. **Date:** 1930s–1950s (multiple buildings over decades)
3. **Location:** Sampaloc, Manila (urban campus)
4. **Client:** Far Eastern University (Dr. Nicanor Reyes)
5. **Program:** education campus — administration, classrooms, gymnasium, chapel (a master-planned ensemble)
6. **Site:** dense urban block; tropical climate (Manila heat + rain + typhoons)
7. **Context:** prewar art-deco/modernist moment; the first Filipino modernist campus
8. **Design concept (parti):** **modernism adapted to the tropics** — functional plans, expressed structure, and a systematic climate response: brise-soleil/sun-breakers, deep overhangs, cross-ventilated classrooms
9. **Materials:** reinforced concrete (exposed), local finishes; structural honesty (modernist tenet)
10. **Structure:** RC frames; expressed beams/columns — structure as facade rhythm (modernist)
11. **Environmental strategy:** the signature: **sun-breakers/brise-soleil**, overhangs, operable windows for cross-ventilation, shaded walkways/arcades linking buildings — an early "tropical modernism"
12. **Circulation/spatial:** campus as connected ensemble; shaded pedestrian paths between blocks (a tropical campus network)
13. **Construction:** progressive construction over decades; war interruption and postwar completion
14. **Significance/lessons:** Antonio's tropical-modernism thesis decades before "green design" was named — passive cooling via architecture, not machines; National Artist 1976. Lesson: modernism + climate = a Filipino modern language (precursor to Locsin/Mañosa).

## CASE 8 — BAHAY NA BATO (Vernacular-Colonial Type Study, e.g., Vigan Heritage Houses, 18th–19th c.)

1. **Architect:** anonymous master builders (friar-era construction tradition)
2. **Date:** 18th–19th centuries (Vigan UNESCO-listed ensemble)
3. **Location:** Vigan, Ilocos Sur (and nationwide variant typology)
4. **Client:** ilustrado/merchant families
5. **Program:** urban dwelling + commercial ground floor (zaguan), living quarters above
6. **Site:** narrow urban lots along the grid (Laws of the Indies planning)
7. **Context:** Spanish colonial urbanism; tropical monsoon climate; seismic risk
8. **Design concept (parti):** **the tropical-adaptive hybrid** — stone below (zaguan: storage/carriage/workshop), timber above (living): mass where security/storage matters, permeability where climate demands
9. **Materials:** volcanic tuff/adobe stone (lower), hardwood timber frame (upper), **capiz-shell sliding windows**, clay tile/pressed-tin roofs
10. **Structure:** masonry ground floor; timber post-and-beam upper frame — a hybrid structure
11. **Environmental strategy:** the passive masterclass: **ventanillas** (small below-window vents), **sliding capiz panels** (light + breeze with privacy), **volada** (cantilevered gallery shading the street), deep eaves; high ceilings for stack-effect; raised floor for flood/ventilation
12. **Circulation/spatial:** zaguan → stair → sala (reception) → cuartos; the *caida* (landing hall); axial-colonial hierarchy
13. **Construction:** local materials, craft joinery (timber without nails in places), earthquake-aware assembly
14. **Significance/lessons:** the canonical PH answer to "how do you build for this climate?" — every tropical-design question traces back to it; UNESCO recognition validates it; adaptive-reuse lessons for heritage practice (RA 10066 context). Lesson: vernacular = accumulated climate wisdom; study it before re-inventing.
