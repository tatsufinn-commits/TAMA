## 1. CURTAIN WALLS — the modern high-rise envelope

**Definition:** a **non-structural exterior wall** hung on the building frame; carries only its own weight + wind loads → transfers them to the structure via anchors. It does NOT support floor/roof loads. (Trap: curtain wall = load-bearing — never.)

**System types (the exam table):**

| System | How it's built | Cost | Install speed | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Stick-built** | Mullions/transoms assembled on-site piece-by-piece, glazed in place | Medium | Slow (more site labor) | Classic system; flexible; quality depends on site work |
| **Unitized** | Pre-fabricated off-site as complete units, hoisted & fixed | High | Fast (less site labor) | Preferred for high-rise (speed + quality control); needs tight tolerances |
| **Semi-unitized** | Hybrid: large panels pre-assembled, small pieces on-site | Medium–High | Moderate | Balance of cost & speed |
| **Structural glazing** | Glass bonded with structural silicone; minimal/no visible mullions | High | Slow/precise | "All-glass" look; maximum daylight; needs engineered silicone |

**Key components (vocabulary):** mullion (vertical member), transom (horizontal), vision glass, spandrel panel (opaque zone hiding slabs), pressure plate/caps, anchors, gaskets, thermal breaks, curtain-wall-to-structure connections (allow movement — seismic/thermal).

**Performance requirements (PH):** wind load (typhoons!), water penetration resistance (rain + typhoon), air infiltration, thermal performance (low-E glazing — tropical file), seismic drift accommodation (frame movement without glass breakage).

**PH practice notes:** high-rise PH buildings use unitized systems increasingly; typhoon wind zones drive mullion sizing; water-tightness testing is standard. (Labeled: practice observation, not a code requirement.)

## 2. PRESTRESSED CONCRETE — beyond the basics (extends existing file)

**Mechanism (why prestress?):** concrete is strong in compression, weak in tension. Prestressing **pre-compresses** the section so that service loads produce net compression or low tension → cracks controlled → longer spans, thinner sections.

**Pre-tensioning (recap + depth):**
- Tendons tensioned **before** concrete placement, against **abutments/prestressing bed** (up to ~200 m long); concrete bonds to tendons; after hardening, tendons cut → compression transferred **by bond**.
- Typical products: **precast hollow-core slabs, bridge girders, precast beams** (plant-made).

**Post-tensioning (recap + depth):**
- Tendons in **ducts/sheaths**, stressed **after** concrete hardens **against the concrete itself** (end anchorages); force transferred **by bearing/anchorage**; ducts then **grouted** (bonded system) or left unbonded (greased, for later adjustment).
- Typical uses: **high-rise flat-plate slabs (long spans without interior columns), transfer girders, parking structures** — cast-in-place site work.

**Key numbers (from cited lecture + existing vault):**
- Min concrete strength at transfer f′ci ≈ **20.5 MPa (3,000 psi)** per ACI 318 (already in vault file — verified).
- Tendons: high-strength strands/wires (much higher strength than ordinary rebar).
- Losses: elastic shortening, creep, shrinkage, relaxation — accounted in design (advanced — label as such).

**Trap:** pre-tensioning = "before" + bond transfer (plant); post-tensioning = "after" + anchorage transfer (site). The existing vault file's distinction is confirmed; this adds the *mechanism* (bond vs bearing) and *products/uses*.

## 3. OTHER ADVANCED SYSTEMS (BT5 territory)

- **Tilt-up concrete:** walls cast flat on site, tilted into place — warehouses/big-box (PH: some industrial).
- **Prefabricated/precast framing:** columns/beams/panels from plant → fast erection; PH housing (BP 220 context) and podium structures.
- **Steel space frames / long-span systems:** trusses, space grids — auditoriums/airports.
- **Composite systems:** steel deck + concrete (composite slabs), steel-concrete columns.
- **Insulated concrete forms (ICF)** and **modular/container construction** — emerging (label: contemporary practice).

## 4. COMMON MISTAKES

| Trap | Fix |
|---|---|
| Curtain wall carries floor loads | Non-structural; only self-weight + wind |
| Stick-built vs unitized confusion | Site-assembled vs factory-assembled units |
| Pre- vs post-tensioning transfer mechanism | Bond (pre) vs anchorage/bearing (post) |
| Tendons = ordinary rebar | High-strength prestressing strands |
| Ignoring wind/typhoon in PH curtain walls | Wind zone drives mullion sizing & water-tightness testing |
| Grouting = optional | Bonded post-tensioning requires grouting (corrosion protection + bond) |
