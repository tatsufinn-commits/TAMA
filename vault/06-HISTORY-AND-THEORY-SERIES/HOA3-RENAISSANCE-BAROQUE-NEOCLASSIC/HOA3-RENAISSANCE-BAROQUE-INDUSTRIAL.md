## 2. CANONICAL PERIOD MASTERS & LANDMARK STRUCTURES

### 2.1 Renaissance Masters & Treatises
* **Filippo Brunelleschi (1377–1446):** Re-invented linear perspective; constructed the **Florence Cathedral Dome (Santa Maria del Fiore)** without wooden centering using a double-shell herringbone brick system.
* **Leon Battista Alberti (1404–1472):** Author of *De Re Aedificatoria* (1452); formulated the classical definition of architectural beauty as *"a harmony of all parts in whatever subject it appears, fitted together with such proportion and connection, that nothing could be added, diminished, or altered, but for the worse."* (Designed **Palazzo Rucellai**, **Sant'Andrea in Mantua**).
* **Donato Bramante (1444–1514):** Master of High Renaissance centrality; designed the **Tempietto of San Pietro in Montorio** (1502) and original central-plan St. Peter's Basilica.
* **Andrea Palladio (1508–1580):** Author of *I Quattro Libri dell'Architettura* (1570); master of symmetrical triadic villa layouts (**Villa Capra / Villa Rotonda**, Vicenza).

### 2.2 Baroque Dynamic Spatiality (Bernini vs. Borromini)
* **Gian Lorenzo Bernini (1598–1680):** Theatrical high papal Baroque; designed the grand elliptical **St. Peter's Piazza Colonnade** (1667) symbolizing the motherly arms of the church, and the bronze **St. Peter's Baldacchino**.
* **Francesco Borromini (1599–1667):** Master of geometric manipulation; designed **San Carlo alle Quattro Fontane** (undulating convex-concave facade and complex geometric coffered dome).

### 2.3 19th-Century Industrial Revolution & Structural Ferrous Technology
* **The Crystal Palace, London (1851):** Designed by Sir Joseph Paxton; prefabricated modular cast iron and sheet glass building ($564\text{m} \times 124\text{m}$) assembled in 9 months.
* **Eiffel Tower, Paris (1889):** Designed by Gustave Eiffel; $324\text{m}$ puddle-iron open-lattice structural tower engineered to resist aerodynamic wind pressures.
* **The Chicago School & The Skyscraper (1880s–1890s):**
  * William Le Baron Jenney: Designed the **Home Insurance Building, Chicago** (1885), the world's first metal-skeleton high-rise building.
  * Louis Sullivan: Designed the **Wainwright Building (St. Louis)** and formulated *"Form follows function"*.

## 3. SOCRATIC EXAM SIMULATION (BOARD-STYLE SCENARIOS)

#### Question 1 (The Chicago School & Skeleton Construction):
Which historic 10-storey building in Chicago, completed in 1885 and designed by William Le Baron Jenney, is recognized in architectural history as the world's first skyscraper utilizing a complete internal structural iron/steel skeleton to carry the entire weight of the floors and exterior masonry cladding?
- A) Wainwright Building
- B) Home Insurance Building
- C) Monadnock Building
- D) Carson, Pirie, Scott Building
> **Correct Answer: B**  
> **Distractor Trap Analysis**:
> * The **Home Insurance Building** in Chicago (1885) by William Le Baron Jenney is universally recognized as the pioneer of iron/steel skeleton frame construction, freeing exterior walls from carrying load-bearing weight.
> * The **Monadnock Building** (Option C) was the world's tallest load-bearing masonry building ($1.8\text{m}$ thick brick walls at the base).
> * The **Wainwright Building** (Option A) by Louis Sullivan came in 1891, articulating the tripartite aesthetic expression of the high-rise.
