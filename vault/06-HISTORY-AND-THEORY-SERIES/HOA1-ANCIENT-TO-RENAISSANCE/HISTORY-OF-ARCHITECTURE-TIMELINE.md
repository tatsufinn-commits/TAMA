## 2. THE 5 CLASSICAL ORDERS (GREEK & ROMAN)

| Classical Order | Origin & Proportions | Capital Details & Entablature | Iconic Architectural Precedent |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Greek Doric** | Height $= 4–6 \times D$; no base; masculine, sturdy. | 20 flutes with sharp arris; plain echinus and square abacus; frieze with alternating **triglyphs and metopes**. | **The Parthenon, Athens** (Ictinus & Callicrates, 447 BC). |
| **Greek Ionic** | Height $= 8–9 \times D$; feminine grace. | Fluted shaft with fillets; capital adorned with twin **spiral volutes**; continuous carved relief frieze. | **Erechtheion, Athens** (Mnesicles, 421 BC). |
| **Greek Corinthian** | Height $= 10 \times D$; slender, opulent. | Bell-shaped inverted echinus adorned with two rows of carved **acanthus leaves** and diagonal volutes. | **Choragic Monument of Lysicrates** (335 BC). |
| **Roman Tuscan** | Height $= 7 \times D$; simplified Etruscan Doric. | Smooth un-fluted shaft, simple ring base, plain frieze; pure utilitarian strength. | Roman military storehouses, forum colonnades. |
| **Roman Composite** | Height $= 10 \times D$; Roman imperial luxury. | Superimposed hybrid capital combining **Ionic volutes above Corinthian acanthus leaves**. | **Arch of Titus, Rome** (81 AD). |

| Historical Monument | Period & Location | Master Architect / Builder | Structural Innovation & Architectural Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **The Pantheon** | Roman Imperial (Rome, Italy) | Emperor Hadrian (125 AD) | $43.30\text{m}$ ($142\text{ ft}$) diameter unreinforced pozzolanic concrete coffered dome with a $9.0\text{m}$ open central **oculus**; stepped rings resist lateral hoop tension. |
| **Hagia Sophia** | Byzantine (Istanbul, Turkey) | Isidore of Miletus & Anthemius of Tralles (537 AD) | First monumental use of triangular spherical **pendentives** to support a $31.0\text{m}$ circular dome atop 4 massive square piers. |

## 4. SOCRATIC EXAM SIMULATION (BOARD-STYLE SCENARIOS)

#### Question 1 (Brunelleschi's Structural Dome Technique):
In constructing the massive octagonal dome of Santa Maria del Fiore in Florence without full-span wooden falsework centering, what ingenious brick masonry technique did Filippo Brunelleschi employ to prevent inward collapse during construction?
- A) Pre-tensioned steel tie rods embedded inside the stone ribs.
- B) Herringbone (*spina di pesce*) bricklaying pattern with an inner and outer double-shell system.
- C) Flying buttresses anchored to adjacent campanile towers.
- D) Lightweight volcanic pozzolanic pumice concrete mixes.
> **Correct Answer: B**  
> **Distractor Trap Analysis**:
> * Brunelleschi utilized vertical locking bricks in a **herringbone (*spina di pesce*) pattern**, mechanically interlocking horizontal brick rings so each course became a self-supporting compression ring during construction. He paired this with a double-shell structural profile.
> * Lightweight volcanic pumice (Option D) was Roman Pantheon technology, not Florentine Renaissance brickwork.
