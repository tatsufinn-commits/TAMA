## 1. KEVIN LYNCH’S FIVE ELEMENTS OF THE CITY (*The Image of the City*, 1960)

1. **Paths:** Channels along which the observer customarily, occasionally, or potentially moves (streets, walkways, transit lines, canals, expressways).
2. **Edges:** Linear elements not used or considered as paths by the observer; boundaries between two phases, linear breaks in continuity (shores, railroad cuts, edges of developments, walls).
3. **Districts:** Medium-to-large sections of the city, conceived of as having two-dimensional extent, which the observer mentally enters "inside of," and which are recognizable as having some common identifying character.
4. **Nodes:** Strategic points in a city into which an observer can enter; intensive foci to and from which he is travelling (junctions, places of a break in transportation, crossing of paths, town squares).
5. **Landmarks:** Point-references considered external to the observer; simple physical elements which may vary widely in scale (towers, domes, monuments, prominent hills).
